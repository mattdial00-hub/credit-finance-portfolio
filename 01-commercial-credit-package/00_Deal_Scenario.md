# Deal Scenario: Brazos Bend Fabrication, LLC

> Fictional scenario created for portfolio demonstration.

## The request

Relationship manager referral to credit, October 2026.

Brazos Bend Fabrication, an existing customer since 2019, is requesting:

1. **$2,800,000 equipment term loan** to purchase a fiber laser cutting system and two CNC press brakes, total cost $3,500,000. Seven-year amortization, fixed rate.
2. **Increase to its revolving line of credit** from $1,000,000 to $1,500,000 to support receivables and inventory as volume grows.

The owners will inject $700,000 of equity: $300,000 of company cash and $400,000 contributed personally by Daniel Reyes.

## The borrower

| | |
|---|---|
| Entity | Texas limited liability company, taxed as a partnership |
| Founded | 2004 |
| Business | Structural and custom metal fabrication |
| Location | 48,000 sq ft leased facility, Gulf Coast region of Texas |
| Employees | 68 |
| End markets (FY2025) | Commercial construction 40%, midstream energy 35%, industrial OEM 25% |
| Concentration | Largest customer 14%; top five 46% |
| Backlog (9/30/2026) | $7.9MM (about five months) |
| Statements | CPA-reviewed, fiscal year ending December 31 |

## Owners and guarantors

**Daniel Reyes (60%), founder and CEO.** Certified welding inspector. Fifteen years at a large Houston fabricator before founding the company.

**Angela Brooks (40%), COO.** Joined in 2011 as controller; became COO and partner in 2018. Runs estimating, purchasing, and finance.

## Why the equipment

Brazos Bend outsources laser cutting today. That adds cost, lead time, and quality risk on tight-tolerance energy work. Bringing it in-house lets the shop control scheduling and capture margin it currently pays to a vendor. Management estimates a 2.0% gross margin benefit.

## Existing relationship

- Term loan, $1.84MM outstanding at FYE 2025, paid as agreed
- Revolving line, $1.0MM commitment, $780K outstanding at FYE 2025
- No covenant waivers or late payments; statements delivered on time every year

## History worth knowing

The company's largest revenue decline was 9%, in 2020, when energy customers paused projects. It stayed profitable that year.

## What credit needs to answer

1. Can the business service the new debt from historical cash flow, without relying on projected savings?
2. Do the guarantors add real support?
3. Is the collateral adequate for total exposure?
4. How much downturn can the company absorb before it can't pay?
5. What structure and covenants protect the bank?
