## Demand drivers

Structural and custom fabricators sell into three main channels: commercial and industrial construction (structural steel, stairs, platforms, miscellaneous metals), energy (pipe supports, skids, process platforms), and OEM components. Each is cyclical. Construction follows interest rates and developer financing; energy follows commodity prices and midstream capital budgets.

A mix across all three, like the borrower's, smooths results but does not remove cyclicality. In a broad downturn all three can soften together.

## Current conditions

Regional manufacturing sentiment strengthened over the summer of 2026. In August, the Dallas Fed's general business activity index for Texas manufacturing rose to 11.6, the highest level since January 2025, with production and new orders also accelerating and firms optimistic about the next six months.

The trend was uneven earlier in the year. In June, output growth had slowed to a below-average pace, and one fabricated metal respondent to the Dallas Fed survey expected softer demand in the second half while describing business as still strong overall.

**Credit takeaway:** near-term demand supports the borrower's growth assumptions, but the analyst should not extrapolate one strong quarter. The projection uses 6% growth, below the company's 11.3% three-year CAGR.

## Input costs and tariffs

Steel is the largest single input. Section 232 tariffs on imported steel and aluminum reached as high as 50% on many products in 2025 and remain there in 2026. In October 2026, U.S. steel prices climbed to their highest level in more than four years as tariffs restricted foreign supply, raising costs for domestic manufacturers.

Analysts have noted that fabricated metal products see the strongest price pass-through because of their high steel content. S&P Global reported in mid-2026 that tariffs raised input costs for some downstream manufacturers and compressed their margins.

**What matters for credit is pass-through ability.** A fabricator that quotes fixed prices on long jobs absorbs steel spikes. One that uses escalation clauses and prices job by job passes them on. The borrower's gross margin rose from 26.0% to 27.2% during this period, which is evidence its pricing is working.

## Competitive landscape

The industry is fragmented and regional. Freight costs keep most fabricators competing within a few hundred miles. Work is won by bid, so differentiation comes from certifications (AISC, ASME), turnaround time, tolerance capability, and relationships with general contractors and engineering firms. In-house capabilities like laser cutting shorten lead times, which is a real competitive advantage in bid situations.

## Labor

Skilled welders, fitters, and CNC operators are hard to hire. Wage pressure is elevated: the Dallas Fed's wages and benefits index was around 31 in July 2026, well above neutral. Automation such as fiber lasers and CNC press brakes reduces dependence on manual labor per unit of output, which supports the equipment request.

## Key risks for this borrower

1. **Downturn in construction or energy.** Mitigated by end market mix and backlog.
2. **Steel price spikes on fixed-price work.** Mitigated by escalation clauses and stable inventory days.
3. **Tariff policy changes.** Could cut either way: lower tariffs reduce costs but increase competition from imported fabricated steel.
4. **Labor availability.** Mitigated in part by automation from the new equipment.
